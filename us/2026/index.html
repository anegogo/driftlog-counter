Count them from photos

Add photos or a Google Maps Timeline export and the page finds 2026 in them, month by month. Add older years as well and it can tell which states were new.

On iPhone: tap Choose photos, pick from your Photo Library and leave “Location Is Included” on (it is by default). A few hundred photos at a time works well, and each batch adds to the count (Add more photos under the card). Exported your Google Maps Timeline? Tap Choose photos → Choose Files and pick location-history.json (how to export it).

On a computer you can also drop photos or a whole folder on this box. Nothing is uploaded: files are read inside your browser. Check the network tab, or switch off Wi-Fi once the page has loaded. It keeps working.

    How this works

    What goes on the card
    The states with a photo or a location point dated 2026, how many of them turn up in each month, and how many were new. No dates, no place names below the state, and no photo.
    What makes a state new
    Nothing you added from that state is dated before 2026. So the page reads every year in your files, not only this one, and when the files hold nothing older than 2026 it calls nothing new. A state you went to years ago without taking a located photo will look new; untick Say which states were new and the card leaves that out.
    Dates
    A photo's date is the one its camera wrote, in whatever time zone the camera was set to. A video's is the creation time in its file, in UTC. A Timeline point takes the date of the stretch of the timeline it sits in. A trip over New Year can land a day on either side.
    What counts as a state
    The fifty states, the same as the states counter counts. Washington, D.C. is filled in on the map and named under the card, but it is not a state and is not in the number. The state you live in counts too, if you took a located photo there this year; Edit this year's states takes it out if you would rather count trips only.
    State lines
    From the US Census Bureau's 1:500,000 boundary file (2024), drawn to within about 65 metres between states and along the borders with Canada and Mexico. A photo taken closer to a line than that can land on the wrong side; one from a beach, a pier or a ferry goes to the nearest state.
    Privacy
    No file and no coordinate leaves your device. The only requests the page makes are for its own HTML and scripts, fonts and the file of state lines, the same fixed files for everyone, so it keeps working with the network switched off once it has loaded. There are no analytics and no cookies. Share hands your share sheet the image and this page's address; the address carries nothing about your list.
